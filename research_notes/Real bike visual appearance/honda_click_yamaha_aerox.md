# Real-world visual appearance: Honda Click 125i and Yamaha Aerox 155 (Philippine market, current to Nov 2026)

Scope and reading guide for the report writer:
- Official hondaph.com / yamaha-motor.com.ph spec pages could NOT be fetched (404/403 or not indexed). Everything below comes from Honda PH press releases (hondaph.com news), Philippine media (Top Gear PH, Inside Racing, Carmudi, Wheels.ph, Motopinas) and aggregators (ZigWheels, Motodeal, Carmudi). Aggregators disagree in places; conflicts are flagged.
- **Honda naming is confusing. Two different Click bodies appear in the PH market data:**
  1. **"Click125i" (2018 body)**: launched Sept 2018 as Click125i/150i, refreshed with colours through 2021. ZigWheels/Carmudi still list a "Click 125i 2026" with this body's dimensions (80/90-14 and 90/90-14 tires) and the 2021 colour set. Whether Honda PH still sells it new in late 2026 is unconfirmed.
  2. **"Click125" (all-new body, Dec 2022)** with the **Feb 2026 "Game Changer Upgrade" facelift** (Standard / Smart Edition, sales from 10 Feb 2026). This is the current Honda PH flagship Click, and the one a modeller should treat as "current generation". Honda dropped the "i" from the name in the 2022 press material.
- **Yamaha:** the current PH bike is the **all-new "Aerox" (the "Mio" prefix was dropped), launched 1 Aug 2025**, in Standard and SP trims. The older "Mio Aerox 155 / S ABS" (2017 to about 2024) is the previous generation. The user brief's "Aerox S 155" corresponds to the old S/S ABS trim; the 2025 equivalent is the **SP**.

## 1. Overall silhouette and body design language (front apron, headlight/DRL, tail light, side panels, floorboard, seat, grab rail, proportions)

### Takeaway
Click: a compact, low, step-through "mini-sport" scooter with a sharp, angular LED face. The 2022 body was already a redesign of the 2018 "alien-esque" front, and the 2026 facelift keeps the silhouette and adds side-fairing vents/ducts, a redesigned grab bar and a subtly reworked headlight. Aerox: a larger, taller maxi-sport scooter with a full-cowl/fairing look, an extended angular headlamp housing, a short smoked windscreen, pointed LED tail lamp, wide seat and an XMAX-like front fascia; the 2025 PH model keeps the previous generation's silhouette but sharpens the details.

### Inferences
- Click 2026: same low, compact "Click" shape as 2022; model the face with a V-shaped/angular LED headlight and slim position-light signature, add small vent/duct cut-outs on the side fairings and a restyled (more sculpted) grab bar to distinguish 2026 from 2022.
- Aerox: should read as a tall-fronted, full-fairing "mini-maxi" — wider and longer than the Click by about 6 cm and 3 cm, with a pronounced beak, a dual (twin-element) headlamp cluster under an extended housing, and a pointed, angular tail lamp. The sources do not say whether the 2025 headlamp is a split twin-pod or a single housing; the Thai page says dual projector LED with DRL and ZigWheels PH says "dual LED headlamps", so twin lamps is the best-supported reading.
- The PH "all-new" Aerox looks like an evolution of the 2017-style Aerox (sources say it keeps the silhouette), so reference imagery of the old Aerox 155 is a decent structural guide.

### Gaps
- No source describes the exact headlight/DRL geometry for either bike (no official photos readable in this environment). Tail-light shape for the Click is not described at all beyond "LED".
- Grab rail shape (Click 2026 "redesigned") and the Aerox rear grab rail are not described.
- Whether the old 2018-body "Click125i" is still sold new in PH in late 2026 is unconfirmed.
- Official Yamaha PH / Honda PH pages were not accessible; all visual descriptions are from press text, not from direct image inspection.

## 2. Key dimensions (length, width, height, wheelbase, seat height, ground clearance, wheel and tire sizes, weight)

### Takeaway
Click (2026 Click125, PH): about 1,919 x 687 x 1,075 mm, wheelbase 1,280 mm, seat 769 mm, ground clearance about 131-132 mm, kerb weight 111-112 kg, 14-inch wheels with 90/80-14 front and 100/80-14 rear. Aerox (2025, PH): 1,980 x 710 x 1,170 mm, wheelbase 1,350 mm, seat 790 mm, ground clearance 145 mm, kerb weight about 130 kg, 14-inch wheels with 110/80-14 front and 140/70-14 rear.

**Yamaha Aerox 2025 (PH)**

### Inferences
- The Aerox is about 61 mm longer, 23-31 mm wider (710 vs 679-687), about 95-108 mm taller, 70 mm longer in wheelbase and 21 mm higher in seat than the Click, with a much wider rear tire (140 vs 100 mm section). The tall/wide rear tire and 145 mm clearance are strong visual differentiators.
- Both use 14-inch wheels; the Click's tires are narrow-profile (high sidewall, 80-aspect) while the Aerox's rear is a low-profile 70-aspect 140 section.
- For the modeller, the 2026 Click should use 687 mm width / 1,075 mm height (new body) rather than the older 679/1,062 values.

### Gaps
- Official Honda PH / Yamaha PH spec sheets were not accessible; dimension values are from Top Gear, Motodeal, Carmudi, ZigWheels. Honda 2026 gives 131 vs 132 mm clearance and 111 vs 112 kg weight depending on the source.
- No separate Aerox Standard vs SP dimension differences are published (ZigWheels notes one table for both).
- Aerox weight: 130 kg (PH, ZigWheels) vs 127 kg (Thai, wet) — different markets/conditions, not necessarily a conflict.

## 3. Wheel and tire look, brakes, suspension, exhaust/muffler

### Takeaway
Click: black cast wheels (introduced 2022), wide-ish tubeless tires, front disc with a wavy-edge rotor and rear drum (combi-brake, no ABS), telescopic fork and a single rear shock, short muffler with an aluminium end cap. Aerox: 14-inch cast wheels (SP: red-painted rims), discs front and rear (230 mm), 30 mm-tube telescopic fork, twin rear shocks with a sub-tank/reservoir on the right, single-channel ABS on the SP only; exhaust not described in sources.

- Aerox: wide 14-inch cast wheels (likely multi-spoke), Standard likely body-matched or dark and the SP red; calipers on both discs; twin rear shocks with remote reservoir/sub-tank. Colour of Standard rims is not documented.
- The Aerox has a visibly heavier rear stance (140/70 tire) than the Click (100/80).

### Gaps

- Mag spoke count/pattern for both bikes is not described in text; need photos.
- Sources disagree on Aerox rear shock layout wording ("two shock absorbers" vs "sub-tank suspension"), so exact layout (twin shocks, one with a sub-tank) is an inference.
- Rim colour for the Aerox Standard and the Click 2026 is not stated.
